## Options

- `scene.route((a, "o2"), (b, "o1"), kind="waveguide", path_type="smooth")`: a waveguide with real bends.
- `steps=[{"x": ...}, {"y": ...}]`: the corners a route must pass, for a fold or a lane.
- `kind="electrical"` or `kind="fiber"`: wires and fibres between electrical or fibre ports.
- `render_physical=True`: draw a part at its true size instead of its schematic size.

## What it supports

Grating and edge couplers, MMIs, Y branches and directional couplers, rings and racetracks, MZIs and
modulators, AWGs and star couplers, Bragg gratings, detectors, lasers and SOAs, and the
[electronics](/electronics/) beside them.

## How it works

Parts are drawn schematically by default: thin features are widened so they read on a page. Every chip part
stands on one open process stack, the GDSFactory generic one: the slab and the doping share the core's base,
contacts rise from the slab to M1, and pads sit on top. In a schematic the oxide is drawn thinner; a scene
with a foundry stack uses its heights. Routes attach to ports, never to the art. Before you call a layout done, render it and look: a route that crosses another,
or a port left open, still draws cleanly.
