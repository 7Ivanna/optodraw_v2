optodraw computes the steady spectrum of a chip from closed forms of its parts, or from S-parameters that you
import from a simulation. The drawn electronics compute their signals too. Each number says where it comes from:
measured, literature, simulated or model.

## Options

- `spectrum(scene, start_nm=1540, stop_nm=1560, points=2001)`: the power at each port where light ends.
  `s.of(part, "o1")` gives one curve, and `s.notes` says what it rests on.
- `signal_chain(scene, t_ns=t, inputs=[((ring, "heater"), ramp)])`: every drawn signal on the grid `t`.
  `chain.at(adc, "dout")` gives one, and `chain.show(scope)` draws it on the scope.
- `Sourced(4.08, Source("literature", "James et al. (2023)"))`: a number with its source.
- `SParameters.load("coupler.npz", source=...)`: a table from SAX `.npz` or Touchstone `.sNp`.
  `part.model = table` replaces the closed form.
- `to_sax(scene, wavelengths_nm=w)` from `optodraw.sax_export`: the circuit as a SAX netlist.

## What it supports

Closed forms for each part that `registered_types()` lists. Tables carry TE only. optodraw warns when the largest
singular value of a table is above 1 + 1e-6, and refuses it above 1.05 or outside its wavelengths. The chain
models detectors, TIAs, amplifiers, ADCs, DACs, laser drivers, modulators and heaters, with first-order poles and
no noise.

## How it works

The engine adds complex fields in the `exp(-iωt)` convention of SAX, with the group index. The free spectral range
is right, and the absolute resonance position is a model value. The chain solves the light once per time sample.
SAX gives the same spectrum to 1e-9.
