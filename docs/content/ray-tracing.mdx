The ray tracer sends rays through the optics in a scene. Lenses bend them by their focal length, mirrors
fold them, splitters branch them, apertures clip them and detectors stop them.

## Trace it

The beam expander of Curd et al. (2015), Fig. 1A: a lens of 45 mm then a lens of 400 mm, 445 mm apart.
`trace_bundle` sends a bundle of rays from the laser; each ray becomes a beam in the drawing.

## Options

| Argument | Does |
|---|---|
| `rays` | How many rays in the bundle |
| `width` | The bundle's width at the source |
| `divergence_deg` | Spread from a point, like a lamp or a fibre tip |
| `exit_length` | How far a ray runs after the last part |
| `mode` | `"physical"` (true hit points) or `"schematic"` (snaps to part centres) |

`trace_branched(scene, (laser, "out"))` traces one ray that splits at every splitter.

## What it supports

Thin lenses and curved mirrors (in the plane and in height), flat mirrors, splitters and dichroics by
wavelength, refraction by index, gratings by order, apertures, periscopes, retroreflectors and absorbers.
In the editor, Trace re-traces live as you move a part.

## How it works

Each part declares its optical faces: a segment, a kind and its numbers (focal length, index, cutoff). The
tracer finds each ray's next face, applies it, and carries the ray's height along, so a 3D bundle comes to
a true focus. A part's `optical_interfaces()` returns its faces; `None` lets the tracer build them from the
part's type.

```python
from optodraw.core.raytrace import OpticalInterface

OpticalInterface(p1=(0.0, -5.0), p2=(0.0, 5.0),   # the face, in the part's own frame
                 kind="lens",                      # lens | mirror | beam_splitter | dichroic | refract
                                                   # | grating | aperture | filter | relay | retro
                                                   # | absorber | schematic
                 efl=30.0)                         # the focal length; a mirror has f = R/2
```

## Where it falls short

One wavelength per trace. Lenses and curved mirrors are thin elements: no thick-lens aberrations. The bundle is geometric, with no Gaussian waist or diffraction limit. Plain mirrors reflect from both sides. For lens design, use [Optiland](/reference/compatibility/) as the engine.
