## Options

- `scene.route((pd, "e1"), (pad, "e1"), kind="electrical", width=1.2)`: a wire between electrical ports.
- `scene.route((die_pad, "e1"), (board_pad, "e1"), kind="wirebond")`: a gold wire bond, an arc in 2D and a loop in 3D.
- `Resistor`, `Capacitor`, `Inductor`, `OpAmp`, `Ground`, `BiasTee`, `RFMixer`: circuit parts. `value="10 kΩ"`
  prints after the label.
- `FiberArray(count=8, pitch=0.127)`, `SMAConnector()`, `PCB(style="plain", color=...)`: the package around a die.
- `ElectricalPad(width=..., height=..., port_side="bottom")`: a bond pad facing its wire.
- `GSGProbePad`, `HeaterPad`, `MetalRoute`: probes, heaters and drawn metal.
- `TIA`, `ADC`, `DACDriver`: circuit blocks drawn on the chip; `TIABlock`, `EIC`, `PCB` for the board.

## What it supports

Every part's electrical ports have the kind `electrical`; `Laser`, `Sample` and `Cryostat` have `e1`, `Photodiode`
`e1` (anode) and `e2` (cathode). A route can carry signal facts (impedance, bandwidth, bit rate), and `validate_electrical_chain` reports a
mismatch such as RF into a DC input. `check_figure` reports a wire that ends more than 0.5 units from its drawn port.

## How it works

Electrical routes use the waveguide router; in 1D a wire turns square, and parts step into line so it runs
straight or turns once. Pads and metal keep their true size. Circuit blocks are drawn as symbols. A circuit part keeps one plan of its ports and lines, and the 1D, 2D and
TikZ drawings read it. In 3D it is a real package: a chip resistor, an SOIC op amp. A die draws over its board.
